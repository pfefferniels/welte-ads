The early ads are lists. The first, printed in Munich in 1905, names 25
pianists, composers and conductors and promises that "what the great
masters of our day achieve can no longer perish". Many of these names do
not last in the ads: Conrad Ansorge, Egon Petri and Édouard Risler are
named only there, Józef Śliwiński and Max Pauer not after 1911. Alfred
Grünfeld is named in eight ads before the war and in two after it,
Frederic Lamond in six and in one. Paderewski, Teresa Carreño and Eugen
d'Albert, named most, stay.
