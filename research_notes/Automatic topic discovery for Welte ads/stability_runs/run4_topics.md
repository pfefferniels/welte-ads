# Run 4: inductive topic discovery, Welte-Mignon advertisements (1905–1930)

Corpus: 195 ads, read in the given shuffled order (run4/ads.json only). Procedure: generation (running topic list, about 55 raw topics) → refinement (merges, two levels, 40 rhetorical topics + 14 functional categories) → assignment → check pass (keyword searches in all languages for each topic, then verification against the rules; about 60 assignments added and a few removed).

Counts = number of ads in which the topic occurs (an ad can carry many topics). 181 of 195 ads carry at least one rhetorical topic; the remaining 14 are purely functional (concert programmes, sale notices, agency notices): ad044, ad047, ad048, ad050, ad054, ad063, ad084, ad085, ad115, ad127, ad152, ad166, ad168, ad173.

## A. The reproduction: fidelity and presence

### A1 Faithful reproduction (100)

**Rule:** The ad explicitly claims that the instrument reproduces the pianist's playing exactly, faithfully or naturally, down to touch, nuance, dynamics, tempo, pedalling or 'every detail' (an accuracy/fidelity claim, not the mere statement that it 'reproduces the playing of' pianists).

**Examples:**

- ad082 [no]: “gjengit av The Welte Mignon reproduktionsapparat med en absolut nøiagtighet saavel hvad angaar tempo, anslag og tonkvalitet som den correcte nuancering av foredraget” (gloss: reproduced by the Welte Mignon reproducing apparatus with absolute accuracy as regards tempo, touch and tone quality as well as the correct nuancing of the performance)
- ad144 [en]: “Every shade and degree of accent, rhythm, and tone is duplicated perfectly and exactly as the Artist rendered it—nothing is added, nothing lost.”

### A2 Illusion of the living artist (35)

**Rule:** The ad claims that the reproduction cannot be told apart from the live artist, or that it creates the sensation that the pianist is present or seated at the piano ('as if he sat at the instrument', 'close your eyes', 'the illusion is complete', 'its playing is the artist's playing').

**Examples:**

- ad180 [de]: “Schliesst man :-: die Augen, so ist die Täuschung vollkommen.” (gloss: If one closes one's eyes, the illusion is complete.)
- ad029 [en]: “you have but to close your eyes to feel the pres-ence of the master at the keys.”

**Ads:** ad005, ad006, ad009, ad010, ad013, ad016, ad023, ad029, ad030, ad032, ad033, ad035, ad037, ad040, ad049, ad075, ad099, ad104, ad107, ad110, ad117, ad122, ad126, ad139, ad143, ad144, ad145, ad151, ad172, ad176, ad179, ad180, ad181, ad186, ad190

### A3 Soul and personality of the artist (26)

**Rule:** The ad says the instrument captures the artist's soul, spirit, personality, feeling, fire or musical essence — the inner, personal element beyond technique (references only to 'individual touch' or 'individual characteristics' go to A1).

**Examples:**

- ad035 [en]: “The Welte-Mignon Autograph Piano Is the Living Soul of the Artist”
- ad180 [de]: “Die auswechselbare Notenrolle ist die Musikseele des Meisters.” (gloss: The interchangeable music roll is the musical soul of the master.)

**Ads:** ad006, ad009, ad010, ad012, ad016, ad020, ad023, ad030, ad032, ad033, ad035, ad040, ad041, ad069, ad100, ad110, ad113, ad139, ad148, ad151, ad170, ad175, ad180, ad181, ad199, ad201

### A4 Art, not mechanism (28)

**Rule:** The ad sets the instrument apart from mechanical or automatic players (pianola, 'self-playing instruments', the old electric piano), insists that it plays humanly and is no surrogate, or claims genuine artistic value or significance for it.

**Examples:**

- ad175 [de]: “Viele glauben vielleicht, es handle sich um ein mechanisches, rein maschinenmässig spielendes Instrument, korrekt und präzis im Vortrag, aber ohne Wärme, Seele und Ausdruck! Nichts wäre irriger, als eine solche Ansicht.” (gloss: Many perhaps believe it is a mechanical instrument playing purely like a machine, correct and precise in performance but without warmth, soul and expression! Nothing could be more mistaken than such a view.)
- ad002 [en]: “Until the Welte-Mignon made its appearance all the piano-playing 'instruments aimed at making the interpretation of a piece of music mechanically correct; the Welte-Mignon caught the great masters in the act of playing”

**Ads:** ad002, ad005, ad006, ad008, ad009, ad010, ad033, ad035, ad100, ad102, ad130, ad131, ad134, ad148, ad149, ad151, ad159, ad160, ad169, ad170, ad171, ad172, ad175, ad178, ad180, ad192, ad193, ad212

### A5 The artist hears himself (16)

**Rule:** A pianist is reported or quoted as hearing his or her own recorded playing reproduced and recognising it as their own (or being deceived by it), or as authenticating the recordings as true to their playing.

**Examples:**

- ad170 [de]: “Raoul Pugno: Es ist das erste Mal, dass ich mich selbst gehört habe. Ich habe mein Spiel genau wiedererkannt.” (gloss: Raoul Pugno: It is the first time that I have heard myself. I recognised my playing exactly.)
- ad041 [en]: “the maestro instantly recognized his own touch, and after the jest was explained, he had the exquisite pleasure of hearing, for the first time, the outpouring of his own artistic soul.”

**Ads:** ad014, ad015, ad037, ad039, ad041, ad075, ad082, ad100, ad117, ad143, ad144, ad151, ad170, ad177, ad205, ad212

### A6 Phantom hands and the invisible pianist (18)

**Rule:** Text or illustration shows keys moving with no one seated, ghostly or semi-transparent hands or pianist, or a piano playing by itself before listeners (the slogan 'The Master's Fingers on Your Piano' alone does not count).

**Examples:**

- ad104 [en]: “WATCH the keys of your piano moving, though no one sits before it!”
- ad033 [en]: “No one sits at the piano. Only the phantomhands of the masters touch the keys”

**Ads:** ad011, ad014, ad015, ad016, ad020, ad021, ad026, ad027, ad028, ad029, ad030, ad032, ad033, ad040, ad080, ad104, ad105, ad197

## B. Authority, endorsement and proof

### B1 Testimonials of famous musicians (43)

**Rule:** The ad quotes, or closely paraphrases, a named musician or other notable person (conductor, composer, novelist, court) praising the instrument.

**Examples:**

- ad008 [de]: “Mignon ist für mich das unerreichte Ideal aller Musik-Instrumente , eine Erfindung von unschätzbarer Bedeutung für Lehrer, Schüler , Publikum und alle Künstler. Max Reger.” (gloss: For me Mignon is the unattained ideal of all musical instruments, an invention of inestimable importance for teachers, pupils, the public and all artists. Max Reger.)
- ad140 [en]: “WILLIAM BACKHAUS, the celebrated Pianist, writes:—“For me this is one of the wonders of the world.””

**Ads:** ad005, ad008, ad009, ad010, ad014, ad015, ad032, ad035, ad037, ad041, ad049, ad065, ad075, ad082, ad088, ad089, ad100, ad101, ad116, ad117, ad130, ad131, ad139, ad140, ad143, ad148, ad151, ad158, ad165, ad169, ad170, ad171, ad172, ad175, ad177, ad178, ad179, ad185, ad192, ad205, ad208, ad211, ad212

### B2 Collective and institutional endorsement (34)

**Rule:** The ad claims endorsement in aggregate rather than by a quoted individual: 'all great artists', leading music authorities, critics or the musical press, conservatories and other institutions that bought or use it, or testimonials available on request.

**Examples:**

- ad102 [de]: “wurde soeben vom k. k. österreichischen Ministerium für Kultus und Unterricht zu pädagogischen Zwecken für das Wiener Konservatorium angekauft.” (gloss: has just been purchased by the Imperial-Royal Austrian Ministry of Culture and Education for teaching purposes for the Vienna Conservatory.)
- ad099 [da]: “kendt Verden over beundret og anbefalet af alle de største Musiknavne” (gloss: known the world over, admired and recommended by all the greatest names in music)

**Ads:** ad005, ad008, ad009, ad010, ad033, ad035, ad067, ad069, ad073, ad076, ad091, ad092, ad093, ad096, ad099, ad100, ad101, ad102, ad116, ad117, ad130, ad131, ad148, ad151, ad169, ad170, ad172, ad174, ad178, ad185, ad201, ad205, ad210, ad212

### B3 Royal and aristocratic prestige (11)

**Rule:** The ad invokes royalty or aristocracy: royal or imperial owners and listeners, aristocratic audiences, or court-purveyor warrants and imperial insignia attached to the maker or the dealer.

**Examples:**

- ad041 [en]: “When starting on a trip to Norway, the German Emperor ordered a WelteMignon Autograph Piano for his steam yacht.”
- ad209 [es]: “De cómo se reúnen en las mansiones aristocráticas de Europa, sólo para oír la maravillosa e impecable ejecución de obras musicales en la “WELTE-MIGNON”” (gloss: How people gather in the aristocratic mansions of Europe just to hear the marvellous and impeccable performance of musical works on the Welte-Mignon)

**Ads:** ad003, ad010, ad033, ad041, ad070, ad071, ad103, ad165, ad195, ad196, ad209

### B4 Owners' satisfaction and social proof (11)

**Rule:** The ad cites owners or buyers — named owners' letters, a typical buyer's voice ('That's why I bought it') — or the number of instruments placed or families that own one.

**Examples:**

- ad171 [de]: “In den zwei Jahren, seit ich den Apparat besitze, hat derselbe stets sehr gut funktioniert und ist mir wie auch vielen anderen zu einer Quelle des schönsten Kunstgenusses geworden.” (gloss: In the two years since I have owned the apparatus it has always worked very well and has become for me, as for many others, a source of the finest artistic enjoyment.)
- ad029 [en]: “"It brings the world's greatest pianists into my home ... That's why I bought it."”

**Ads:** ad029, ad030, ad040, ad073, ad117, ad165, ad170, ad171, ad172, ad176, ad205

### B5 Star pianist in the spotlight (16)

**Rule:** The ad is built around one named celebrity pianist — headline, portrait, career story, recording-session photograph or anecdote — rather than a list of names.

**Examples:**

- ad011 [en]: “Paderewski WILL Play Again Thanks to the wizardry of Hardman WelteMignon(licensee) Reproducing Pianos the art of Paderewski is imperishable .”
- ad192 [fr]: “OSSIP GABRILOWITSCH PIANISTE - VIRTUOSE ET DIRECTEUR DE LA SYMPHONIE DE DETROIT Jouera pour vous du Chopin, du Schumann, du Tschaïkowsky” (gloss: Ossip Gabrilowitsch, virtuoso pianist and conductor of the Detroit Symphony, will play Chopin, Schumann and Tchaikovsky for you)

**Ads:** ad002, ad007, ad011, ad014, ad015, ad034, ad037, ad039, ad041, ad112, ad116, ad120, ad134, ad187, ad192, ad208

### B6 Proof in public performance (8)

**Rule:** The ad cites success in a public concert, theatre, exhibition or on the stage as proof of the instrument's quality, or stages a public demonstration (e.g. alongside a live pianist, or one piece by two pianists) designed to prove it.

**Examples:**

- ad046 [fr]: “Le grand succès du Steinway-Welte au dernier concert au Conservatoire a prouvé son éclatante supériorité dans la reproduction de jeu des maîtres du clavier” (gloss: The great success of the Steinway-Welte at the last concert at the Conservatoire has proved its brilliant superiority in reproducing the playing of the masters of the keyboard)
- ad137 [en]: “M. Lambert's feat of playing alternately with the great Master's own actual reproduced playing will be of great interest to music lovers”

**Ads:** ad022, ad025, ad046, ad136, ad137, ad138, ad159, ad184

### B7 Hear it and be convinced (12)

**Rule:** The ad challenges readers to listen for themselves, to compare it with rival instruments, or to test whether they can tell it from a live artist, asserting that hearing is believing.

**Examples:**

- ad026 [en]: “Welte-Mignon (Licensee) Hear it—in comparison.”
- ad186 [de]: “Sie werden das Spiel dieses Reproduktions-Klaviers wohl kaum von demjenigen eines Künstlers unterscheiden können. Interessiert Sie der Versuch?” (gloss: You will hardly be able to tell the playing of this reproducing piano from that of an artist. Are you interested in trying?)

**Ads:** ad005, ad017, ad026, ad027, ad028, ad033, ad035, ad040, ad117, ad175, ad186, ad202

### C4 Technology and the secret process (31)

**Rule:** The ad foregrounds how the instrument works: electric or pneumatic drive, roll mechanism, the (secret, 'photographic') recording process, patents, technical features, or comparisons with photography or the phonograph. A product label such as 'electric reproducing piano' on its own does not count.

**Examples:**

- ad033 [en]: “Through this secret photographic process possessed only by Welte-Mignon,* available only to Welte-Mignon* - the artist's playing is actually photographed”
- ad180 [de]: “Der Mechanismus wird von einer Notenrolle, dem Gehirn des Apparates, gelenkt.” (gloss: The mechanism is guided by a music roll, the brain of the apparatus.)

## D. Home, use and ownership

### D1 The masters at your command at home (61)

**Rule:** The ad promises the playing of the great pianists in the buyer's own home or on his own piano, whenever he wishes (including the recurring slogan 'The Master's Fingers on Your Piano'); hearing them in the dealer's showroom does not count.

## Notes on difficult boundary decisions

- **A1 vs A3 (fidelity vs soul).** Claims about touch, nuance, dynamics, pedalling, "individual characteristics" or "personal touch" were coded A1 only. A3 needs an explicit appeal to the artist's soul, spirit, personality, feeling, fire or "das Persönliche" as something beyond technique. Several Scandinavian and German formulas ("individuelle Karakteregenskaber") were therefore left in A1.
- **A2 vs A5 (deceived listener vs deceived artist).** A2 covers indistinguishability and presence for any listener. A5 is reserved for pianists who hear and recognise their own reproduced playing. "It deceives even the artists themselves" (ad143, ad144) and Grünfeld's friends feeling he sat at the piano (ad075) were coded in both.
- **A6 vs D1.** The recurring Licensee slogan "The Master's Fingers on Your Piano" was coded D1 (masters on your own piano) and not A6. A6 needs ghost or phantom hands, a transparent pianist, or a piano playing with nobody seated, in the text or the picture.
- **B1 vs B2.** A named, quoted statement counts as B1 (the quoted court letter in ad165 included). Aggregate claims ("all great artists", conservatories, critics and press, "testimonials on application", "Künstler-Urteile" catalogues) count as B2. Conservatory adoption also triggers E3 when teaching use is spelled out.
- **B3 Royal prestige.** Royal or aristocratic use of the instrument (ad165, ad041, ad033, ad209) was grouped with court-purveyor warrants and imperial eagles. These belong to Welte (ad003, ad103) or to the dealers (ad010, ad070, ad071, ad195, ad196). This was a judgement call: the warrants are dealer credentials, but they lend courtly prestige to the purchase, so they were kept here and also listed among the functional dealer credentials.
- **C2 vs C3.** Wonder vocabulary (wonder, marvel, miracle, magic, astonishing, "beyond belief") goes to C2. "Invention" goes to C3 only when it is qualified (genius, epoch-making, extraordinary, greatest invention) or the ad frames novelty or progress ("sensational novelty", "after many failures", "only instrument of its kind in Canada"). "Beundransvärd uppfinning" (admirable invention) was coded C3 only. A bare mention of "this invention" was not coded.
- **C4 Technology.** Product labels such as "electric reproducing piano" were not coded. Statements about how the instrument works were coded: "by means of electricity", "plugs into any light line", the photographic process, patents, roll as the "brain" of the apparatus. The plug-in statements also count as D8 (effortless operation).
- **C6 World renown vs distribution.** "Agencies throughout the world" or "represented in all larger places of the world" describe the sales network and were treated as functional. Only claims of fame or admiration were coded C6.
- **D1 needs the home.** Being able to hear the masters "whenever you wish" in the dealer's studio or showroom (ad039, ad115) was not coded D1.
- **D4 and the pictures.** Illustrations of evening dress, rococo or eighteenth-century salons, and elegant parties were coded D4. Plain interiors with a piano were not. D7 needs at least two listeners in the picture. D6 needs a woman as the central figure or as the person addressed.
- **D11 merges two candidates.** A separate "evocative musical scenes" topic (storm, gaucho dance, garden of long ago) occurred in 7 ads, all of which also stress repertoire variety. It was merged into D11.
- **D10 vs functional prices.** Bare prices, terms and bargains are functional. D10 is used only when the ad argues accessibility ("within everyone's means", "out of your reach—emphatically, no!", value compared with an ordinary player).
- **Small topics kept.** D12 (5 ads) and E7 (4 ads) are above the threshold of about 3 and are rhetorically distinct, so they were kept. Dropped as too rare (2 ads or fewer): comparison with the phonograph (ad013, ad033, now under C4), the "unused piano" appeal (ad097, ad135, under D8 and functional trade-in) and allegorical decoration such as muses, fauns and lyres (not a selling argument).
