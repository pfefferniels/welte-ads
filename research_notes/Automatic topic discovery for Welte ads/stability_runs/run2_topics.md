## A. Fidelity and the artist's presence

### A1 Faithful reproduction (98 ads)

**Rule.** Code when the ad asserts, with an explicit accuracy qualifier (exact, faithful, true, actual, original, authentic, natural, perfect, flawless, 'down to the smallest detail'), that the instrument reproduces the real playing of the artists. A bare 'plays the great pianists' without such a qualifier does not count.

**Examples.**

- ad037 (de): “Getreue Wiedergabe des Spiels berühmter KLAVIER-Virtuosen” — *Faithful reproduction of the playing of famous piano virtuosos*
- ad144 (en): “Every shade and degree of accent, rhythm, and tone is duplicated perfectly and exactly as the Artist rendered it—nothing is added, nothing lost.”

### A2 Touch, nuance and individuality (57 ads)

**Rule.** Code when the ad specifies what is preserved beyond the notes: touch, nuance/shading, dynamics, tempo, rhythm, phrasing, pedalling, tone colour, technique, or the pianist's individuality, personality or characteristic traits (incl. ### A3 Illusion of the artist's presence (32 ads)

**Rule.** Code when the ad claims the reproduction creates the illusion that the artist is physically present, or is indistinguishable from live playing: 'as if the artist were seated at the piano', 'close your eyes', 'deceives even the artists', 'you can hardly tell the difference', 'living performances in your home'.

**Rule.** Code when the ad claims that the artist's soul, spirit, feeling or inner life is captured or transmitted ('living soul of the artist', 'Musikseele', 'played his soul into the Welte', 'his spirit will pervade the room', 'what a great artist feels and thinks').

### A5 Art, not mechanism (29 ads)

**Rule.** Code when the instrument is set apart from mere mechanism: it plays humanly/artistically, has genuine artistic value or significance, is 'not to be confused' with merely mechanical players, or has lost 'every trace of the mechanical'.

## B. Time: immortality and the lost past

### B2 If only it had existed for Beethoven and Chopin (10 ads)

**Rule.** Code when the ad laments that the invention did not exist earlier, so that the masters of the past (Beethoven, Mozart, Chopin, Liszt, Rubinstein, Bülow …) could still be heard.

**Examples.**

- ad088 (da): “Man maa kun beklage at denne Opfindelse ikke allerede blev gjort tidligere; thi da kunde vi endnu den Dag i Dag høre Beethoven, Mozart og Chopin selv spille” — *One can only regret that this invention was not made earlier; for then we could still today hear Beethoven, Mozart and Chopin play themselves*
- ad131 (en): “The regret of musicians must ever be that this great invention was not brought out in 1809 instead of 1909.”

**Ads.** ad009, ad010, ad088, ad100, ad101, ad130, ad131, ad151, ad175, ad211

## C. Marvel, invention and technology

### C1 Wonder, miracle and magic (47 ads)

**Rule.** Code when the instrument is called a wonder, miracle, marvel, magic or wizardry, or when the listener's astonishment or disbelief is stressed ('unbelievable', 'uncanny', 'struck dumb', 'how astonishing'). The routine adjective 'wonderful/marvellous' alone does not count.

**Examples.**

- ad005 (de): “Das Welte Mignon ist das Wunder der Wunder.” — *The Welte-Mignon is the wonder of wonders.*
- ad029 (en): “It is modern magic.”

### C2 Epoch-making invention and progress (35 ads)

### C5 How it works: technology and recording (28 ads)

**Rule.** Code when the ad explains or showcases how the instrument works: the recording process (photographic recording, the recording session), the electro-pneumatic mechanism, the roll as 'brain', electric drive from a light socket or instead of foot power, special features (full keyboard, fixed valves, expression patents), control devices such as the Musicalle. A product label like 'electric reproducing piano' alone does not count.

**Examples.**

- ad180 (de): “Der Mechanismus wird von einer Notenrolle, dem Gehirn des Apparates, gelenkt.” — *The mechanism is guided by a music roll, the brain of the apparatus.*
- ad143 (en): “The original playing is recorded by a special Photographic process, which only the Welte possesses”

### C6 The name 'Mignon' is too modest (8 ads)

**Rule.** Code when the ad (usually via the Paderewski or Saint-Saëns testimonial) remarks that the modest name 'Mignon' falls short of the instrument's colossal effect or significance, or otherwise makes the name itself a sign of its importance.

**Examples.**

- ad169 (de): “Das einzige, was ich „Mignon“ vorzuwerfen habe, ist nur sein Name, weil er weder der gewaltigen Wirkung dieses Instrumentes, noch seiner kolossalen Be— deutung entspricht.” — *The only thing I have to reproach 'Mignon' with is its name, because it matches neither the tremendous effect of this instrument nor its colossal significance.*

## Notes on difficult boundary decisions

1. **Fidelity (A1) vs. nuance/individuality (A2).** Both concern the core reproduction claim and overlap heavily. A1 needs an explicit accuracy qualifier ("exact", "getreu", "natuurgetrouw", "actual playing", "Originalspiel", "flawless"). A2 needs a named performance parameter (touch, nuance, tempo, pedalling, phrasing, tone colour, technique) or an appeal to the pianist's individuality or personality. "Individual interpretation" counts for A2. "Interpretation" on its own does not (so ad114 is A1 only).
2. **Art not mechanism (A5) vs. rivals (D2).** The early German and Swedish ads say the Mignon "is not to be confused with other players that play only mechanically" and coded both topics: A5 for the art-versus-machine opposition, D2 for the competitive claim. A5 alone is used where there is no comparison, only a claim of artistic value ("hohen künstlerischen Wert", "konstnärligt värde", "vraiment artistique"). The mere product label "Artistic Player Piano" (ad114) and the stock German phrase "Kunstgenuss" (artistic enjoyment) were not coded A5.
3. **Wonder (C1) vs. invention (C2).** C1 is affective: marvel, magic, astonishment. C2 is historical-technical: epoch-making, greatest invention of the age, the end of a progress story. Many testimonials carry both. The generic adjectives "wonderful", "marvellous", "maravilloso" and "underbar" were *not* coded C1. Exclamations staged as reactions ("Marvellous! Yes, but …") and "uncanny/astonishing fidelity" were coded.
4. **The name "Mignon" (C6).** This motif became a topic only in the check pass. Paderewski's complaint that the name is too modest, and Saint-Saëns's "bescheidener Name", recur in 8 ads in five languages. It could also be read as a sub-motif of D3 (testimonials). It is kept separate because it does a distinct rhetorical job: understatement used as a superlative.
5. **Fame (D6).** Only claims of international renown, sensation, awards or public success count. The routine epithet "the famous Welte-Mignon action" was left out (it is very frequent in the Auto Pneumatic ads). "Beroemde/afamada fábrica" (renowned factory) went to D7 (standing of the firm).
6. **Heritage (D7).** The age, tradition and size of the *maker* (founded 1832, Michael Welte's history) and of the *dealer* ("Ye Olde Firme", "Grundlagt 1885", "largest store in Canada") are merged here. This is an ethos appeal, not functional address data.
7. **Original (C4).** The legal footnote "*Licensed under the original Welte-Mignon patents" was not coded. "The Original Auto-Piano" / "De originele autopiano's" (Auto-Piano Co. brand claims, ad055, ad066, ad068) *were* coded, because the ad itself makes the originality claim.
8. **Slogan "The Master's Fingers on Your Piano".** It was coded both A7 (phantom hands) and E1 (the masters at your piano), even when it is the only trace of these motifs in an ad.
9. **Illustrations.** Figure descriptions were coded for A7 (piano playing with nobody at it), E4 (relaxed listening by one to three people: armchair, sofa, family at the fire), E5 (gatherings of several guests, often in evening dress), E6 (elegant upper-class settings), E8 (a woman as focal figure at the instrument) and E10 (dance and garden scenes). An empty room with an unplayed piano (ad182) was not coded A7.
10. **Immortality (B1).** This includes the implicit cases where a deceased artist is listed among those you can hear (ad004 "Reisenauer †", ad052 "wijlen Teresa Carreño") and artists preserved "at the zenith" of their careers. The more specific lament for unrecorded past masters is B2.
11. **Home (E1).** This includes statements that the instrument is meant "exclusively for the private home" (Reutemann) as well as the at-your-command motif. Plain invitations to hear the masters *in the showroom* are functional.
12. **Hearing is believing (D9) vs. demonstrations.** "Call and hear it" or "daily demonstrations" alone are functional. D9 needs hearing to be framed as proof or conversion ("you have but to hear it", "Hear it—in comparison", "Interessiert Sie der Versuch?").
13. **Affordability (E11) vs. price/bargain.** Clearance sales, discounts and second-hand offers are functional (BARG/PRICE). E11 needs an explicit appeal that the instrument is within ordinary people's reach, or that music is brought to everyone.
14. **Education (F1).** Concerts held in a conservatory hall (ad045, ad046) were not coded F1. F1 needs a learning or teaching claim, or institutional purchase for study.
15. **Concert notices.** Programmes that bill a reproduced performance as "played by Grünfeld/Grieg …" (e.g. ad167, ad077, ad168) were treated as functional (DEMO/PROG/ROSTER). They received no rhetorical code even though they implicitly stage the absent pianist as performer. Programmes where a composer plays his own work were coded F3 only when the text says so explicitly (ad200 "Executat per son autor").
16. **National pride (F4).** Only explicit appeals were coded. An implicitly local repertoire (Spanish composers in the Barcelona programme ad200, Vianna da Motta in Rio, ad119) was not.
17. **Dropped candidates (fewer than ~3 ads, or absorbed elsewhere).** The instrument as a (Christmas) gift (ad028, ad170) was dropped. A live pianist performing alongside the reproduction (ad136, ad137) went to functional/F2. The phonograph comparison (ad013, ad033) went to D2/C5. The photograph metaphor (ad009, ad100, ad131) went to A1/C5. Dealer service and expert mechanics went to D8. The recording session shown in photographs (ad037, ad116) went to C5.
