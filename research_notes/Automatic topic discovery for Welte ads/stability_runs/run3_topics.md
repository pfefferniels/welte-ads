## Reproduction: fidelity and presence

### A1 Faithful reproduction (102 ads)

**Rule:** The ad claims that the instrument reproduces the artist's playing exactly, faithfully, truly or perfectly, or that what one hears is the artist's actual/original playing.

**Examples:**

- ad114 (en): “The Welte gives the absolutely true reproduction of the interpretation of the World's Most Famous Pianists.”
- ad083 (no): “EN ABSOLUT TRO GJENGIVELSE AV VERDENSBERØMTE PIANISTER'S PERSONLIGE SPIL” — *an absolutely true reproduction of world-famous pianists' personal playing*

### A2 Nuance, touch and expressive detail (36 ads)

**Rule:** Names specific performance parameters that are reproduced (touch/attack, dynamics, shading/nuance, phrasing, pedalling, tempo, rhythm, accent, tone colour) or claims that even the finest shadings / smallest details are preserved.

**Examples:**

- ad160 (sv): “återgifver det karaktäristiska hos hvarje pianist, hans anslag, nyansering, tempo- och pedalbehandling” — *reproduces what is characteristic of each pianist: his touch, nuancing, tempo and pedalling*

### A4 Illusion of presence (31 ads)

**Rule:** Claims that the listener would believe the artist is physically present at the piano, cannot tell reproduction from live playing, or recognises at once who is playing ('as if he sat at your piano', 'close your eyes', 'deceives even the artists').

**Examples:**

- ad180 (de): “Schliesst man :-: die Augen, so ist die Täuschung vollkommen.” — *If one closes one's eyes, the illusion is complete.*
- ad029 (en): “you have but to close your eyes to feel the pres-ence of the master at the keys.”

### A5 Beyond the mechanical (21 ads)

**Rule:** Explicitly denies that the playing is mechanical, or contrasts it with 'mechanical', automatic or earlier self-playing instruments in terms of life, warmth, soul or artistry (living, human, no cold regularity, not a surrogate).

**Examples:**

- ad180 (de): “Welte-Mignon spielt menschlich, deshalb künstlerisch. Ihm haftet nichts von der kalten Regelmässigkeit eines automatischen Klaviers an.” — *Welte-Mignon plays humanly, therefore artistically. It has nothing of the cold regularity of an automatic piano.*
- ad134 (en): “This instrument faithfully reflects the master's technique without the slightest indication of mechanical blemish.”

## Authority and testimony

**Rule:** Quotes or reports praise or approval by named musicians (pianists, composers, conductors), or claims in general that the great artists praise, recommend or have 'pronounced it perfect'.

## Time: immortality and the lost past

### C1 Preserved for posterity (39 ads)

**Rule:** The playing is preserved forever or for future generations, made immortal or imperishable ('Instrument of the Immortals'); includes deceased or retired artists who can still be heard.

## Wonder and technology

## Notes on difficult boundary decisions

- **Fidelity cluster (A1–A5).** Generic fidelity ("exact", "faithful", "true", "the artist's actual/original playing") is A1. A2 needs a named performance parameter or "finest shadings / smallest details" (every A2 ad is also A1). A3 needs explicit individuality, personality, style or soul; plain "interpretations" is not enough. A4 is about the listener being deceived about physical presence; "realism almost beyond belief" stays A1 (and D1). A5 needs an explicit contrast with "mechanical", automatic or earlier self-players, or an explicit denial of mechanicalness.
- **A5 versus G3.** A5 is the argument "living, not mechanical". G3 is competitive positioning (better than / unlike other instruments or brands, beware cheap makes) plus invitations to compare or test. Statements like "has nothing in common with other self-playing instruments" or "surpasses all mechanical instruments" get both codes.
- **Endorsement versus roster.** A bare list of recording artists is functional (roster). B1 needs a quote or an explicit claim of praise or approval ("pronounced perfect by the greatest artists", "recommended by the leading music authorities"). B3 needs a single star or a few stars made the centrepiece (headline, portrait, biography, anecdote). Rows of portraits (ad022–ad024) were counted as B3 because the faces are the visual focus.
- **B2 (artists hear themselves)** covers only an artist hearing or authenticating his or her own recording. The general claim that the reproduction "deceives even the artists themselves" (ad143, ad144) was coded A4, not B2.
- **B4 composers.** Included are explicit statements that composers recorded ("Componister", "pianists and composers") and programmes marking a composer as performer of his own work (Grieg by Grieg, Sapellnikoff by Sapellnikoff, Leschetizky by Leschetizky, Granados "per son autor"). Rosters that merely contain composers' names (e.g. Strauss, Saint-Saëns) were not coded.
- **B5 merges official recognition and "world renown".** "World-famous / known the world over / sensation abroad" was first a separate topic but was too thin and lexical on its own, so it was merged here. Plain "famous" was not coded. The Vienna Conservatory purchase is both B5 (recognition) and F1 (pedagogy).
- **B6 royal patronage** includes court-supplier titles (Hoflieferant, Hofleverancier, Fournisseur de la Cour) and imperial-eagle emblems, whether they belong to the maker or the dealer, because they work as prestige signals. Dealer founding dates and "largest store" claims were kept as functional ("firm credentials"), as was the maker's bare "Gegründet 1832". The maker's history told as narrative, or priority claims, went to G4.
- **D1 wonder** is restricted to noun or predicate use ("a wonder of the world", "Marvellous! Yes, but…") and to reported astonishment or disbelief. The ubiquitous attributive "wonderful/marvellous/wunderbar instrument" was not coded. "Magic", "wizardry", "phantom", "spirit" and "fear" go to D2. "Summon / zitieren" was coded E2 (at your command), not D2, unless spirits or ghosts are explicit (Zöllner's poem in ad100).
- **D2 visual criterion.** Illustrations of semi-transparent or ghost hands or figures, or of a piano playing with nobody seated *while people listen* (ad197, ad080), count. The rococo room in ad182, where "no one is playing" and no one listens, was not coded.
- **D3 invention** is fairly lexical: any ad that calls the instrument an invention (Erfindung, opfindelse, uppfinning, wynalazek, vinding, invention), a novelty (newest, Nytt!, últimas novedades, new combination instrument) or a stage of technical progress (patents, "after many inventions and many failures") counts. D4 needs an actual explanation of the mechanism or recording, or a photography/phonograph analogy. "Photographic accuracy" (ad017) was counted as an analogy.
- **E1 home** includes the Auto Pneumatic slogan "The Master's Fingers on Your Piano" and statements that the instrument is meant for the private home ("speziell für das Privathaus", "För hem…"). E4 versus E5: guests, hostess or ≥3 adults in a social picture → E4. Family, couple, lone relaxed listener, fireside or "privacy" → E5. Illustrations of hand-playing groups (ad006, ad099, ad156) were still coded E4, since the scene sells the social setting.
- **E8 versus E10.** E8 is about breadth (library size, "whole range", classics and dance). E10 is vivid evocation of what particular pieces conjure. The genre catalogues in the "Phantom Fingers" series (Gaucho dance, Venetian love lyric…) count as E10, not E8. Counts of recorded *artists* ("hundreds of pianists") were not used for E8, to keep it apart from the functional roster.
- **E9 dance** includes ballet accompaniment (ad022, ad025) and dance pieces as the subject of an evocation (Gaucho dance, tango).
- **F3 versus functional prices/terms.** Bare prices, discounts, instalments and trade-in are functional. F3 needs an argument about attainability or value ("within everyone's reach", "out of your reach—emphatically no!", "valued at £1150 … price £575", "at the price of an ordinary Player Piano").
- **F5 versus model lists.** Plain variant lists ("indbygget i Steinway … og som Forsatsapparat") are functional. F5 needs the compatibility or versatility to be put forward as a benefit: fits *any* or *your* piano, available in 112/115 makes, invisible, manual playing kept, several playing modes. The Auto-Piano Co. slogan "The Original Autopiano" (ad055, ad066, ad068) and the licensee footnote "Licensed under the original Welte-Mignon patents" were *not* counted as G4 originality claims.
- **C1** includes implicit signals that a recorded artist has died ("wijlen Teresa Carreño" ad052, "Alfred Reisenauer †" ad004, "certains de ces prodiges sont disparus" ad193) and Paderewski's retirement (ad011). The epithet "inmortal artista" for the living Paderewski (ad208) alone was not coded.
- **G5 national framing** is a small topic (6 ads) kept because it recurs across three countries (German inventive spirit and Baden "heimische Industrie", Canadian honour and Canadian pianist, Hofmann and "American people").
- **H1** is the only purely visual topic. Firm logos and seals with putti or lyres (Dahlström, Nordiska Kompaniet) were excluded. Organ-pipe frames and generic ornament were not coded.
