# Welte-Mignon advertisements – Canada

Transcribed from the scan images, following [conventions.md](conventions.md).

The following apply to every ad below:
- Quotation marks and apostrophes are given as the curly forms they are printed in.
- The wide spacing after a full stop that the compositors used is written as one space.
- Rows of leader dots are reproduced, but the number of dots is approximate.
- The Fraktur "Welte-Mignon" trade mark is transcribed in normal letters. Where a tiny "(Licensee)" is printed in the swash of its M, that is transcribed on its own line.

### CA-02 — Rhythmic Beat of a Gaucho Dance —When Phantom Fingers Play
- **Periodical:** London Evening Advertiser, London, Ontario, Wednesday, 10 December 1924, 61st Year, No. 24142, p. 8
- **Advertiser:** MASON & RISCH, 248 DUNDAS STREET, LONDON
- **Language:** English
- **Transcribed from:** https://www.canadiana.ca/view/oocihm.N_00257_19241210/8
- **Further printings:** The Free Press, London, Ontario, Wednesday, 10 December 1924, p. 4, https://www.canadiana.ca/view/oocihm.N_00400_192412/195. The address line there is set "248 DUNDAS STREET      LONDON", spread across the column with no comma and no full stop. Its scan shows damaged type ("gladly nave", "yoa", "tme"); the text is otherwise the same as far as I compared it. The Advertiser scan is the cleaner one.
- **Notes:** Comparison with the edition's ad028:
  - ad028.xml is the Atlantic Monthly, **December** 1924, p. 108, not September as given in the task. It is the national ad of the Auto Pneumatic Action Co.
  - Headline: the same words, but the Canadian ad is set in title case with an em dash ("Rhythmic Beat of a Gaucho Dance / —When Phantom Fingers Play"). The Atlantic has "Rhythmic beat of a Gaucho dance / -when Phantom Fingers play".
  - Body text: completely different. The Atlantic text begins "Wild abandon of a peasant song; lure of a Venetian love-lyric; simple charm of a plantation melody", contains "What a Christmas gift!", names Paderewski, De Pachmann, Hofmann and Gabrilowitsch, and says "It is invisible and does not interfere with manual playing … obtainable in practically every good make of piano". The Canadian text instead describes the tango scene ("WATCH the keys of your piano moving…"), names "The Mason & Risch Welte-Mignon", and ends with an invitation to the show rooms.
  - Caption: the Atlantic has "…immortal pianists; their glorious genius preserved forever, through the miracle of Welte-Mignon* reproduction." The Canadian caption has a comma for the semicolon, no "glorious", no comma after "forever" and no asterisk.
  - Imprint: the Atlantic has the brochure offer signed "Auto-Pneumatic Action Co. W. C. Heaton, President, 12th Avenue at 51st Street, New York". Its logo reads "Welte-Mignon (Licensee)", followed by "Hear it–in comparison. There's a nearby dealer" and "*Licensed under the original Welte-Mignon patents."
  - The Canadian ad replaces all of this with the dealer address and a dealer logo, "MASON & RISCH / Welte-Mignon / REPRODUCING PIANO", flanked by two faun panels. It has no "(Licensee)" and no licence footnote.
  - Illustrations: the TEI zones of ad028 show a large picture at the top and a small vignette beside the caption, which suggests the same artwork. I could not view the Atlantic scan, because HathiTrust is behind a bot check.
  - Same series as CA-03, which has the same caption, vignette and logo.
